# Addendum 2026-11-15: kscr2, a reconstruction in the loop and a clairvoyant ceiling

`proposal.md` and `addendum_2026-11-11.md` stay as they are. This addendum records what the user decided after kscr1 and
what is fixed in advance for iteration 2 of the knee subproblem.

## What stands

- kscr1 (`kscr1/README.md`, commit 29659bb), dev half, one seed:
  - G_16 pooled +0.077 [+0.058, +0.094] (knee-PD +0.102, knee-PDFS +0.051);
  - floor +0.002 [−0.013, +0.016];
  - swap cost of A +0.139 [+0.104, +0.180].
- kscr1 shows that the adaptive effect is real. What is not known is how large it can get.

## The user's decisions (11-14 and 11-15)

- **From scratch.** "since we know that the training from scratch works we should just stick with that". kscr2 keeps
  kscr1's recipe: from scratch, uniform-noise start, knee PD + PDFS, the dev half.
- **The gap.** "we want to expand the gap between the adaptive sampling method and the static (one step loupe)". A one-step
  static arm joins. G_16 against the twin stays the bar for claiming adaptivity, because only the twin rules out spacing.
- **Complex input.** "I think inputting a complex would be better". kscr2 makes it physical (S below).
- **Reconstruction in the loop.** "why is there no reconstruction inside the loop? Is it just sequential sampling? It
  should be like seqmri". This adds the SeqMRI-style arm S.
- **6 cascades.** "We can reduce our varnet size to 6 cascades." Every kscr2 arm uses 6 cascades.
- **The round.** "Can you run on corehpc". These are the five arms below. The user's other idea, sampling between the
  cascades of one unrolled VarNet, is deferred to kscr3: it changes the reconstructor itself and needs its own twin.

## Why the loop had no reconstruction

- **Phase 0 removed the inherited loop** (proposal section 3, items 1–3):
  - Items 1 and 2 were bugs in our copy of SeqMRI: the mask was detached between steps, and the sampler read only the
    predicted k-space.
  - Item 3 is inherent to SeqMRI. The reconstructor runs on partial masks the final loss never scores, at K× the cost.
  - kscr2 rebuilds the loop with items 1 and 2 fixed.
- **SeqMRI as published** (Yin et al. 2021, arXiv 2105.06460 v2):
  - One sampler and one reconstructor, shared over T=4 steps, with a reconstruction at every step.
  - The sampler reads the measured k-space, the reconstruction's k-space and the mask.
  - The loss is on the final reconstruction only.
  - Setting: single-coil knee, 128², a U-Net reconstructor.
- **A note on P2's A_4_s42_rec.** Its reconstruction channel sat at input index 2, but its widened first-conv weights were
  appended last, so its shared weights read channels shifted by one.
  - Its start was still identical to T_4's, because the residual's output layer starts at zero.
  - Its result (+0.006 dB) stands, as a weak test.
  - kscr2's new channels go last.

## Iteration 2

The recipe is kscr1's with `recon.num_cascades=6`. Every arm uses seed 42 and starts from the same initialisation, and
the twin's code path is unchanged.

| Arm | Its sampler reads | Role |
|---|---|---|
| T_16_s42_kscr2 | its mask, the step, the header, a fixed tensor | the shared control |
| A_16_s42_kscr2 | y ⊙ M_i, as in kscr1 | the current design at 6 cascades |
| S_16_s42_kscr2 | also virtual coils of y ⊙ M_i and of the k-space predicted by in-loop reconstructions | SeqMRI-style, the best shot |
| C_16_s42_kscr2 | the fully sampled k-space, at every step | the clairvoyant ceiling |
| T_1_s42_kscr2 | the one-step twin, i.e. static LOUPE | the one-step static reference |

**S in detail** (`sampler.input=seqmri`):

- **Where the reconstructions are.** Before steps 6, 9, 12 and 14 (0-indexed), the trained VarNet reconstructs y ⊙ M_i;
  later steps read the latest reconstruction.
  - It is the same network, with the same weights, that reconstructs the final mask.
  - The steps sit where kscr1's sampling depended most on the data: 9–13% of draws moved in steps 2–8, against 26–36% in
    steps 13–16 (1-indexed).
  - Reconstructions at R > 100 would carry little.
- **Gradient.** The final loss trains the reconstructor's and the sensitivity net's weights through the sampler's input.
  That is SeqMRI's co-design path.
  - The in-loop reconstruction sees M_i detached, so no gradient path to earlier draws is added beyond kscr1's
    straight-through union. This keeps one change per arm.
- **Loss.** On the final reconstruction only, as in SeqMRI.
- **Complex input.**
  - V=4 virtual coils come from the eigenvectors of the ACS coil covariance.
  - Each is phase-referenced to be real and positive at the ACS k-space peak. Per-coil phase conventions and the
    sensitivity maps' free common phase both cancel.
  - Two sources: y ⊙ M_i, and the predicted coil k-space FFT(S x̃), cut to the acquired extent. Each gives a log magnitude
    (scaled as the k-space channel) and the cos and sin of the phase.
  - That is 24 channels, appended last to the residual's first convolution. Their weights come from their own generator,
    at the widened fan-in bound.
  - Before the first reconstruction, the predicted channels are 0.
- **Cost.** Five VarNet passes per training forward: four in the loop and the final one.

**C in detail** (`sampler.input=full`):

- The data channels (the RSS image and the log coil-RSS k-space) are made from y, not y ⊙ M_i.
- The ceiling is optimistic, because the mask can carry information about unmeasured k-space to the VarNet.

## Readouts (dev half, as in the 11-11 addendum)

- **Steering signals:** the swap cost and the per-step data-swap movement of A, S and C.
- **Beside them:**
  - G = arm − T_16_s42_kscr2 for A, S and C. The kscr1 floor stands in for kscr2's; this round has no new floor twin.
  - A_16 − T_1 and T_16 − T_1: the user's gap against one-step static LOUPE, and the spacing part of it.
  - 6 vs 12 cascades: the kscr2 A and T against kscr1's, reported only. So far it has only been checked on brain.

## How the results will be read (fixed before the runs)

"Clearly above" means two things: the paired per-slice difference's CI excludes 0, and the swap cost rises over A's.

| Outcome | Reading | Next |
|---|---|---|
| S clearly above A, both against T | the in-loop reconstruction is the lever | every-step reconstruction, then the confirmation |
| C well above both | the information exists, but partial data does not reveal it to this sampler | what the early steps acquire |
| C close to A | the sampler's design or training is the limit | residual lr, draws, architecture |
| Everything near +0.1 dB | mask-shape adaptivity is capped here | for scan adaptivity, ACS budget adaptation (roadmap step 2) |

## Validation and launch

- **The validation job.** `validate_kscr2.sbatch` runs on the pinned RTX PRO 6000. It checks:
  - the test suite;
  - a smoke of each arm for 10% of one epoch, built by `arm_args`;
  - scoring with `--limit 120 --gradients`;
  - the twin's nulls;
  - every arm's swap self-check;
  - the positive control on C and S;
  - the shipped commit.
- **The training jobs** wait on it with `--dependency=afterok`. They are cancelled if it fails.
- **Compute estimate:** about 27 GPU-hours in all, until the smoke's epoch times replace it:
  - about 5 h each for T, A and C;
  - about 10 h for S;
  - about 2 h for T_1.

## Deferred to later iterations

- Sampling between the cascades of one unrolled VarNet. It needs its own twin.
- S reconstructing at every step. It fits CoreHPC's 4-day limit.
- Per-step reconstruction losses. These need a twin trained with the same losses.
- A complex-input arm without the in-loop reconstruction.
- The residual's lr and the number of mask draws.
